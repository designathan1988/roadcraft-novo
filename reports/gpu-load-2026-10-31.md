# GPU at 100 %, worse when the camera comes close — causes (2026-10-31)

Scope: `roadcraft-novo-port`, branch `port/roadcraft-features`, HEAD `a3a9c59`. Question: why the game holds the GPU
at 100 % (Windows Task Manager), and why it gets worse as the camera comes close to the ground.

**Method and limits.** (a) = read in the code, with file:line (R5, three r186 in `node_modules/three`, 4.0 in
`lab/roadcraft`). (b) = a measurement already recorded by the project or a statement of an official/technical source
(§9). (c) = inference. Nothing was run in this session: no game, build, budget or probe (the owner's standing order).
No GPU time per pass has ever been recorded for this branch (`src/render/gpuTiming.ts` exists, the probe never
produced its numbers, `MEMORY/PLAN-PROGRESS.md:70`), so the order of the causes by milliseconds is (c).

---

## 0. The answer

1. **100 % is the GPU never finishing a frame inside the display's refresh interval.** The loop asks for a frame at
   every vsync (`renderer.setAnimationLoop`, `src/game.ts:652`; requestAnimationFrame "will generally match the
   display refresh rate", MDN) and draws every one of them while the clock runs, the camera moves or workers deliver
   (`src/game.ts:813-818`). The recorded measurement (b) on an RTX 3060 at 1920×1080, maximum quality, uncapped: frame
   p50 **17.6–33.1 ms in every view**, CPU outside `post.render` under 2 ms (`docs/ARCHITECTURE-AUDIT-2026-10-30.md`
   §12.1–12.2). The budget at 60 Hz is 16.7 ms (6.9 ms at 144 Hz). No view fits, so the GPU never idles.
2. **What grows when the camera comes close** (the part that makes it worse), in the order of the evidence:
   1. Building parts (window frames, sills, railings, lot pieces) and the props' near level are drawn **to a fixed
      distance per type, with no per-instance test of their size on screen** (`src/jobs/parts40.ts:25-28`). A 6 cm
      window-frame bar is drawn to 400 m, where it is **0.2 px wide**. 4.0 drops a bar part under 0.5 px and any part
      under 1 px; R5 did not port that rule. Recorded (b): near the ground the props add **≥ 5.58 M triangles the
      GPU draws**; from the city view, **1.7 k**.
   2. Those props are drawn into the **shadow cascades with the view's cull list** (one indirect list, not culled per
      cascade), running the props' **full vertex program** in every pass.
   3. The **3 shadow cascades (2048² each) are drawn whole in every frame**. The "redraw only when something moved"
      cache in `post.ts` **never engages**: the default wind is 0.2 (`src/render/weather.ts:22,290`), and
      `game.ts:859` treats any wind above 0.001 as "moving". 4.0 keeps a **static shadow cache** per cascade and
      redraws only what moved.
   4. **Every opaque material carries `discard`** (buildings, props, roads, vehicles, terrain). This puts depth
      writes after the fragment shader (late-Z), so at street level, where façades, parts, trees and the window detail
      overlap, the heavy shaders run on hidden fragments. It also forces a fragment shader into every shadow depth
      pass.
   5. The near layers come in by 3D distance: L0 buildings (700 m), the façade detail (150 m), and the grass tufts
      below 140 m eye height (179,200 blades, never culled).
   6. `pixelScale` multiplies every LOD distance by up to 3 on large or high-DPI screens and on ultra.
3. **What does not change with zoom** is the per-pixel floor: GTAO + denoise, cloud shadow + aerial perspective, TRAA,
   CAS with five tone-mapped taps, and bloom with its own TRAA at night. It is paid on every frame, near or far.

## 1. What "GPU 100 %" means in this loop

- (a) `src/game.ts:652` `renderer.setAnimationLoop((t) => this.frame(t))`. three r186 drives it with
  requestAnimationFrame (`node_modules/three/src/renderers/common/Animation.js`).
- (a) `src/game.ts:813-818`: a frame is drawn when the clock runs, input was seen in the last 800 ms, workers are busy,
  it rains, a demolition is active, or the camera moved. It stops only after `STILL_FRAMES` = 40 idle frames
  (`game.ts:95`). In normal play the clock runs, so every vsync is drawn.
- (a) vsync is on in play. `disable-frame-rate-limit` / `disable-gpu-vsync` are set only with `ROADCRAFT_NOVSYNC`
  (`electron/main.mjs:30-34`), a measurement switch.
- (b) MDN: "The frequency of calls to the callback function will generally match the display refresh rate… 60hz…
  75hz, 120hz, and 144hz are also widely used."
- (b) Recorded frame times (RTX 3060, 1920×1080, max quality, uncapped, `ARCHITECTURE-AUDIT-2026-10-30.md` §12.1):

  | view (10 km city) | frame p50 (ms) | triangles |
  |---|---:|---:|
  | city | 21.88 | 15,437,425 |
  | district | 28.74 | 23,871,270 |
  | street | 23.80 | 31,393,550 |
  | night | 25.19 | 33,243,444 |
  | max traffic | 30.57 | 28,534,610 |

  The "street" camera of that run faced a façade (D56, since fixed), so its view is not a true street.
- (c) A frame longer than the refresh interval, requested at every vsync, keeps the GPU queue always full: the Task
  Manager shows the busiest engine (3D) at 100 %. A frame cap below the display rate would only hide this by lowering
  the frame rate. The cause is the frame's cost, which §2–§3 break down.

## 2. Causes that grow as the camera comes close

### 2.1 Building parts drawn to fixed distances, with no size-on-screen test (sub-pixel geometry)

- (a) `src/jobs/parts40.ts:25-28`: `PART_DIST` = frames 400 m, boxes (sills, fins, shutters) 600 m, rails 300 m, misc
  400 m, rooftop props 250 m, small props 150 m, lot pieces 800 m, "at 1080p". Each part type has **one level**
  (`propMeshes.ts:532`: `lods: [merge(parts), null, null]`).
- (a) The cull compute keeps an instance when its 3D distance is under its type's end (`props.ts:214`, `:223`). Nothing
  compares the instance's own size on screen. The parts are asked for within `PARTS_RANGE` 928 m × `pixelScale` of
  the camera, measured horizontally (`props.ts:513-516`, `:539`).
- (c, from (a) values) Camera FOV 45° (`cameraRig.ts:125`): at 1080p a metre spans 1080 / (2·tan 22.5°) = 1,304 px at
  1 m. A 6 cm frame bar is 0.5 px wide at 156 m and **0.196 px at 400 m**. `pixelScale` scales the distance and the
  pixel count together, so the ratio holds at any resolution. With uniform density in plan, **85 %** of the frames
  drawn (the ring from 156 to 400 m) have bars under half a pixel.
- (a) 4.0's rule, not ported: `lab/roadcraft/src/render/buildings/partCells.ts:26-27` `BAR_PIXELS = 0.5`. A bar part
  leaves the drawing once its bars span under half a pixel, and any part leaves under 1 px across its diagonal
  (`partCells.ts:454-460`). Levels are chosen by `PART_ERROR_PX = 1.5` (`kit.ts:112`). 4.0's own comment records the
  same defect: "300 thousand window frames of 6 cm bars were drawn to 1-2 km as lines a fifth of a pixel wide, 6 M
  triangles of shimmer (2026-10-20)".
- (b) Measured on 2026-10-31 (`MEMORY/WORKLOG.md`, D81, commit `4393481`, real GPU). three's counter (`tris`) sees
  each indirect draw once; `drawn` reads back what the GPU drew:

  | view | tris (three) | drawn (GPU) | drawn − tris |
  |---|---:|---:|---:|
  | street | 27,557,464 | 33,137,676 | **5,580,212** |
  | district | 21,209,234 | 26,325,324 | **5,116,090** |
  | city | 13,294,708 | 13,296,410 | 1,702 |
  | night | 30,473,168 | 36,082,780 | 5,609,612 |

  drawn − tris is a lower bound on the props' own triangles (they are drawn − tris + their counted share,
  `game.ts:680-684`). It is the clearest measured term that appears near the ground and vanishes from above.
- (b) Why sub-pixel triangles cost more than their pixels: GPUs shade in 2×2 quads. A triangle covering a fraction of
  a pixel still launches a quad, and rasterizer and setup throughput are built for triangles larger than a pixel
  (Fatahalian et al., SIGGRAPH 2010: merging quads of pixel-sized triangles cut shading work "by a factor of eight";
  Karis, *Nanite: A Deep Dive*, SIGGRAPH 2021; MJP: forward rendering "gets overwhelmed by quad overshading"). It also
  breaks CLAUDE.md rule 8 (invisible geometry).

### 2.2 The props reach every shadow cascade with the view's list and the full vertex program

- (a) The props are one indirect draw per kind and level, `frustumCulled = false` (`props.ts:268-270`), fed by **one**
  visible list culled against the **main camera** (plus each caster's shadow sweep, `props.ts:202-212`, `:222`).
- (a) three r186: each cascade is a full `renderer.render(scene, shadow.camera)` (`ShadowNode.js:654`; CSM makes one
  shadow per cascade, `CSMShadowNode.js:175-182`). An object with `frustumCulled = false` is not culled against the
  cascade camera, and an indirect draw reuses the same indirect arguments. So every casting near-level instance in
  view is vertex-shaded once for the view and once per cascade whose layer it is on: the boxes and lot pieces
  (`props.ts:276`) and trees in all 3, small casters in the near one (`post.ts:298-301`).
- (a) The shadow pass keeps the object's own `positionNode` (`Renderer.js:3744`). The props' vertex program
  (`props.ts:313-442`) evaluates, for **every vertex of every kind**, the impostor billboard (hemi-octahedral encode
  and decode, cross products), the sign atlas, the wire sag, the barrier stretch, the wind sway, a `textureLoad` of the
  signal texture and uniform-array lookups, then picks one by `mix`/`step`. All paths run, not only the instance's.
- (a) Defect inside this path: `castU` (`props.ts:166`) marks every kind whose near model is ≥ 0.3 m tall as a caster,
  so off-screen instances of frames and rails (unit model 1 m, `r40/render/buildings/kit.ts:64`) join the list for
  their shadow sweep. Their mesh never casts (`props.ts:276`), so the view pass vertex-shades them for nothing.
- (b) 4.0 for comparison: casters are culled per cascade (`lab/roadcraft/src/render/cellInstances.ts:59-62`). Each
  cascade draws a simplified shape within its texel (`shadowLevels.ts:1-10`: "a frame that redrew the cascades held
  4.24 M triangles of gardens, against 68-105 k in the colour pass"). People and vehicles stop at the middle cascade
  (`sunCascades.ts:100`).

### 2.3 The three cascades are redrawn whole in every frame

- (a) `src/game.ts:859`: `this.post.render(!this.clock.paused || windU.value > 0.001 || this.demolition.moving())`.
  `windU` starts at 0.2 (`src/render/weather.ts:22`) and is set to `windFixed ?? 0.2 + storm * 0.8`
  (`weather.ts:290`), so it is never under 0.001 by default. `moving` is always true, `prepareShadows` sets
  `needsUpdate = true` on the 3 cascades (`post.ts:318-327`), and the "still" path never runs, even with the clock
  paused while the camera moves.
- (a) Each redraw renders the scene into 3 maps of 2048² (`post.ts:89`, `:94`): city cells culled per cascade
  (`cellLayer.ts:270-274`), props as in §2.2, vehicles (`vehicles.ts:66`) and walkers at LOD 1 (`crowd.ts:219`), all
  on layer 0, so all 3 cascades.
- (c) Near the ground the cascades follow the frustum, and the near one is filled with the densest geometry: L0
  buildings, parts, small casters and leaf cards. From above, the near part of the frustum is air.
- (b) Microsoft, *Cascaded Shadow Maps*: the depth maps are "rendered one at a time", with "a null pixel shader…
  because only the depth is needed". Unity HDRP: "Every Frame" redraws a light's shadow maps every frame, while "On
  Enable"/"On Demand" cache them; mixed cached shadows keep static casters cached and draw the dynamic ones each frame.
  Unreal Virtual Shadow Maps cache static pages and redraw what moved. Acton, *CSM Scrolling* (Insomniac, SIGGRAPH
  2012): scrolling the cached map avoids rendering about 70 % of the static geometry.
- (a) 4.0 does all of this (`lab/roadcraft/src/render/sunCascades.ts:33`, `:206`): a "STATIC SHADOW CACHE" per
  cascade, movers drawn over a copy of it, the store scrolled with the view (`:43` `SCROLL_MOST`), whole redraws only
  on change, refit or every 600 frames (`:57`), staggered (`:59-60`), the sun moved in tolerance steps (`:41`).

### 2.4 `discard` in every opaque material: late depth writes and overdraw

- (a) Masks compiled into the shaders: buildings `city.ts:275` (fade dither ∧ inside cut ∧ window hole) and `:277`
  (shadow); props `props.ts:492`; roads `roads.ts:493`; vehicles `vehicles.ts:223`; terrain `terrain.ts:482`. three
  r186 turns `maskNode` into `discard` (`NodeMaterial.js:825-829`), and in the shadow pass into a fragment shader that
  discards (`Renderer.js:3498-3558`). The mask is in the shader for every mesh, also when the fade is 1, nothing is cut
  and no window is open.
- (b) MJP, *To Early-Z, or Not To Early-Z* (2025, measured on an RX 7900 XT): with depth writes on, "the depth write
  can't actually be committed until the pixel shader runs to completion". A discard that never runs raised pixel-shader
  invocations from 440,640 to about 582,000 in a front-to-back test. The effect applies whenever the compiled shader
  contains a discard, "even if no pixel ever ends up being discarded". Microsoft HLSL proposal 0053: such a shader
  "must run in late depth mode due to the discard". Khronos OpenGL wiki (*Early Fragment Test*, read through search;
  the page asked for a bot check): a discard turns off early depth tests on some hardware. NVIDIA GPU Programming
  Guide (G80): double-speed Z-only rendering requires that no texkill (clip, discard) is applied.
- (a) Ordering does not help. three sorts opaques by groupOrder, renderOrder, then object z (`RenderList.js:14-30`).
  The props meshes sit at the origin, so their z is the origin's, and instances inside an indirect draw come in
  `atomicAdd` order (`props.ts:251`). Nothing is drawn front-to-back, and there is no depth prepass.
- (c) Near the ground the depth complexity is high: wall, the detail layer's reveals and panes behind the openings,
  parts in front, trees, vehicles. The hidden fragments run the building shader (branchless procedural windows,
  interior mapping per pixel `city.ts:279-300`, CSM sampling with 5 PCF taps per cascade (`ShadowFilterNode.js:73`)
  and two cascades in the fade bands, PBR + environment). From above the depth complexity is about 1.
- (b) The documented fix is a shader variant without the clip for objects not in transition (Unity's
  `LOD_FADE_CROSSFADE` variant), with the masked ones drawn after the plain opaques (MJP). This was implemented in
  `34b230b` (city meshes) and `31a33cb` (building parts) and reverted two minutes later (`a3a9c59`, `b752023`). No
  reason is recorded in `MEMORY/`.

### 2.5 Near layers that only exist close to the ground

- (a) City L0 to 700 m × `pixelScale` by 3D distance, the façade detail layer (3D openings, sills, heads) to 150 m
  (`city.ts:123`, `:147`; 3D distance with the eye height, `cellLayer.ts:425-433`). Roads L0 to 600 m
  (`roads.ts:106`).
- (a) Grass tufts: 160 × 160 tufts × 7 blades = **179,200** thin triangles, `frustumCulled = false`, whenever the
  eye is under 140 m (`terrain.ts:55-59`, `:688`, `:739`).

### 2.6 `pixelScale` multiplies the distances

- (a) `game.ts:1125-1132`: k = clamp(innerHeight × devicePixelRatio × resolution × detail / 1080, 0.3, 3). Ultra sets
  detail 1.4 (`platform/settings.ts:46`). At 1440p ultra, k = 1.87: every band area grows 3.5×, and the pixel count
  grows 1.78× (`setPixelRatio`, `game.ts:1082`). Scaling distances with resolution is right for screen-space error. It
  multiplies §2.1 because the bands are per type, not per instance size.

## 3. The per-pixel floor (same near and far)

- (a) `post.ts`: scene pass with MRT colour + velocity (`:102-108`). GTAO at half size, 8 samples, plus denoise
  (`:111-131`). Cloud shadow and aerial perspective rebuilt from depth per pixel (`:133-152`). TRAA (`traa.ts`, a copy
  of r186's TRAANode). CAS with 5 taps, each converted by `renderOutput` (tone mapping + sRGB) and `pow(2.2)`
  (`:169-181`). Grade and vignette. At night, bloom over a second TRAA (`:202-212`).
- (b) Commit `1b6c343`: AO at full frame size "ran the GPU to 100 % in the standard photo views". Reverted to half
  size. This floor is real but does not explain the zoom dependence.

## 4. Checked and ruled out

- MSAA: `antialias: true` (`scene.ts:46`) sets `renderer.samples = 4`, but the scene pass is `samples: 0`
  (`post.ts:102`), and three uses 0 samples for a full-screen pass to the screen (`Renderer.js:2641-2652`). No MSAA
  target is drawn per frame.
- Electron flags: vsync is disabled only with `ROADCRAFT_NOVSYNC` (§1).
- AO at full size: already reverted (`1b6c343`).

## 6. The correct direction for each cause (architecture; no quality cut)

1. §2.1: in the props cull compute, reject and level each part by its own projected size, with 4.0's thresholds
   (`BAR_PIXELS` 0.5, 1 px diagonal, `PART_ERROR_PX` 1.5; the scale is already in the key, `parts40.ts:38-41`).
   Sub-half-pixel bars only shimmer, so the 1:1 photo at the switch distance stays the same.
2. §2.2: cull the indirect props per view (the view and each cascade get their own list from the same compute), with
   per-cascade caster shapes (4.0 `shadowLevels.ts`, meshoptimizer at absolute error). Fix `castU` to match
   `mesh.castShadow`.
3. §2.3: a static depth store per cascade with the movers (vehicles, walkers, wind-swayed trees) drawn over a copy,
   scrolled with the view and refreshed by change boxes (4.0 `sunCascades.ts`; Acton 2012; Unity HDRP mixed cached
   shadows; Unreal VSM).
4. §2.4: a mask-free material variant for meshes not fading, cut or opened, drawn before the masked ones (the
   reverted `34b230b`/`31a33cb`; first find why they were reverted).
5. §2.5: frustum-cull the grass grid per tuft in the vertex stage, or per block.
